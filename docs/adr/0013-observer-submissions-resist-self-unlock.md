# Observer submissions resist self-unlocking the comparison gate

The 360 comparison report unlocks only once at least three Observer reads are in (ADR-0003, issue #9), and the model deliberately stores no observer identity. Those two choices sit in tension: with nothing limiting submissions, a Subject could open their own share link and submit three times to self-unlock the report, after which the "anonymous" per-observer drill-down trivially identifies each self-authored read — and the locked-state copy ("so your Observers stay anonymous") overstated a guarantee the gate couldn't make (issue #193). We considered per-IP throttling and storing a hashed dedup key per response, but both record something identity-adjacent about an Observer, cutting against ADR-0003, and IP is a poor, privacy-invasive key behind shared NATs and VPNs. We chose two guards that store no new observer identity: (1) a signed-in viewer who is the Subject is blocked from submitting against their own link — enforced in the server action and surfaced as a friendly note on the observer page — since the Subject is the party with both motive and an authenticated identity we already hold; and (2) a per-link, path-scoped cookie set after a successful write turns a browser's repeat submissions away. Neither is absolute — a Subject can sign out, clear cookies, or use another browser — so this is best-effort, not a hard guarantee; the decision function (`observerSubmissionRejection`) is a pure, unit-tested module and the locked-state copy was corrected to describe anonymity honestly ("nothing records who sent it") rather than promising the gate enforces it.
